Let’s Connect

Case study · Mobile marketplace · 2020

A family of mobile apps that connects agri-input dealers, village retailers and farmers, so seed, fertilizer and crop-protection products can be discovered, enquired about and ordered in one network.

Context

A supply chain that ran on phone calls

Agri-inputs move from dealers to village retailers to farmers, and much of that trade happened over phone calls and visits. Each side had a different need: dealers wanted to find and reach more retailers, retailers wanted to compare dealers and stay in touch with their farmers, and farmers wanted trusted, local advice on what to buy.

The FarMart App family set out to give each side a reason to join, and a structured way to trade once they had. This was the earlier mobile product where I first learned how FarMart’s trade worked, before moving on to FarMartOS and FarMart PRO.

Dealers

Find retailers nearby, promote their stock, answer enquiries and fulfil orders.

Retailers

Source from dealers on one side, serve and market to their farmers on the other.

Farmers

Discover and follow local retailers, ask questions, and get advice from their community.

Design questionHow might we bring a phone-and-paper trade onto an app, when most of the people we need are not on it yet?

Flow 01 · Onboarding

Get in fast, then land where the value is

All three apps start the same way: verify a phone number, fill a short profile, and go straight to the screen that shows why the app is worth it. Everything else in the profile can wait, with nudges to complete it later.

Phone number as identity

One-time-password sign-in instead of passwords, for people who mainly know their phone number.

Value before paperwork

Only the essentials up front; the rest of the profile is unlocked later with completion nudges.

New and returning differ

On the dealer app, first-time users get a map-first, welcoming view; returning users go straight to the list they work from.

Flow 02 · Invites

Invite people who aren’t on the app yet

A network app is empty until both sides are on it. So anyone could add their existing contacts, from the phone book or by hand, whether or not those contacts had the app. People already on the app get a notification; everyone else gets an SMS with a download link, and the invitation waits for them when they arrive.

Reach beyond the app

SMS carries the invitation to people who haven’t installed anything yet.

Consent on both sides

Nobody joins a network without reviewing the profile and accepting.

Show who’s reachable

When sending a message, the recipient list shows who is on the app and who will get an SMS.

Flow 03 · Transactions

Structured messages instead of open chat

The team considered free chat between dealers and retailers, then chose structured messages instead. A promotion, an enquiry, a quotation and a purchase order each became a short template. That simplified the process, let quotations from several dealers line up against one enquiry, and turned an accepted price straight into an order.

Comparable offers

Quotations from several dealers line up against the same enquiry, newest first.

The right person confirms

The retailer confirms delivery; the dealer confirms payment. Each step has one clear owner.

Trust runs both ways

Both sides rate each other, so reputation is earned in each direction.

Pattern · Retailer app

One retailer, two directions

The retailer buys from dealers and sells to farmers, often in the same hour. Rather than two separate apps, the retailer app was specified with two modes: one facing dealers (enquiries, quotations, orders) and one facing farmers (promotions, groups, feeds, orders), switched from a single control.

Flow 04 · Produce collection

The same network, running the other way

Inputs flow from dealers to farmers. Produce flows back. A collection module in the retailer app let FarMart use its retailer network to gather produce from farmers for buyers, with field staff checking quality before pickup.

What this page doesn’t claim yet

The flows above follow FarMart’s 2020 product specifications, and include only flows that are specified end to end. Payments, delivery tracking, subscriptions and feed ranking were parked or undecided at the time, so they aren’t shown. Which flows shipped, and exactly which screens I designed in each app, are still being confirmed. There are no usage numbers on this page.

Diagrams on this page are conceptual and made for this portfolio. They show flows and design approach, not FarMart’s screens, data, message copy or internal business rules. FarMart was a multi-team product: product, engineering and operations colleagues shaped this work with me.
